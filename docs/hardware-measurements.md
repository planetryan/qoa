Generated by `tools/hardware_check.py` with qoa 0.4.0.

## Qubit programs: QOA, Qiskit Aer and IBM device models

Each program is exported with `qoa export --to qasm`, loaded by Qiskit, and run on both simulators (20000 shots each). **TVD** is the total variation distance between the two outcome distributions (0 = identical; with this many shots, sampling noise alone gives about 0.005). Then the circuit is transpiled for real IBM devices (optimisation level 3) and run on a simulator with each device's calibrated noise: **ideal mass** is the share of noisy shots that land on the outcomes the ideal program gives at least 1% of the time (for Grover, that is the success rate), and **TVD noisy** is the distance from the ideal distribution.

## Optical programs: QOA and Quandela's Perceval

For linear optics (beamsplitters, phases, single photons, photon counting), QOA gives the network's single-photon transfer matrix (one photon sent into each input), and Perceval, the software for Quandela's photonic processors, simulates the multi-photon input through that matrix (SLOS back end). **max |Δp|** is the largest difference between the two output distributions.

| network | modes | photons in | outcomes | max |Δp| |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hong-Ou-Mandel, 50:50 beamsplitter | 2 | [1, 1] | 2 | 6.7e-16 |
| Mach-Zehnder with phase pi/3 | 2 | [1, 0] | 2 | 0.0e+00 |
| 6-mode interferometer, 3 photons (boson sampling) | 6 | [1, 1, 1, 0, 0, 0] | 50 | 6.9e-16 |
